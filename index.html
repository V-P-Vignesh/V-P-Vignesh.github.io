About

Vignesh Vijayalakshmi Palanisamy

PhD researcher at IIT Palakkad, working where surveying, BIM and asset management meet the problem of invisible infrastructure.

My research spans the full capture-to-model pipeline for buried and built infrastructure — field instruments, reality capture, and the data frameworks that make the records usable. I work hands-on with GPR, terrestrial and SLAM LiDAR, GNSS, and augmented and virtual reality, and I build the results into BIM and digital-twin models.

The step I care most about is the one usually skipped: deciding how far a piece of subsurface data can actually be trusted, and carrying that confidence through into a working digital twin of the city. Alongside buried utilities, I have published on bridge and road asset condition using LiDAR, and I teach and co-train BIM and digital construction — from a national BIM programme for public-sector engineers to IndiaSkills competitors.

I am based in Palakkad, Kerala, and I am looking for postdoctoral positions and research collaborations in subsurface data, reality capture and digital twins.

Research interests

What I work on

My work runs from deciding what to investigate first, through how to capture it, to trusting and modelling the result as one connected city.

Subsurface utility localisation & mapping

Locating buried utilities and reconciling as-laid geometry with record data using GPR, electromagnetic locating, LiDAR and GNSS.

Subsurface data confidence & quality

Frameworks that score how reliable a piece of utility data is, by how, when and with what method each point was captured.

Remote sensing for buried assets

Satellite imagery and vegetation response (Sentinel-2 NDVI) to detect buried pipeline corridors on greenfield sites before excavation.

Reality capture & field visualisation

Terrestrial and SLAM LiDAR, point-cloud processing, and AR/VR for capturing and reviewing buried and built infrastructure.

Digital twins for infrastructure

Bringing buried and surface assets into connected BIM and digital-twin models that carry both the geometry and the confidence behind it.

Infrastructure asset condition

LiDAR-based temporal damage assessment of bridges and pavements, with efficient scan-area planning to make inspection repeatable.

Transforming pavement inspection: optimized terrestrial laser scanner placement and data-driven damage assessment
V. V. Palanisamy, S. Venkatachalam, V. Gopi Charan | International Journal of Construction Management | pp. 1–18
Q1 · IF 3.4
doi.org/10.1080/15623599.2026.2613966
The rapid growth of global road infrastructure necessitates proactive maintenance strategies to overcome the limitations of conventional inspection. This study introduces a "Scan Area Planning" framework for deploying Terrestrial Laser Scanning (TLS) in flexible-pavement distress detection. Using mathematical models and interactive graphs, it optimises scanner placement for sub-millimetre accuracy; crack-detection algorithms are compared for computational efficiency, and pothole assessment uses convex-hull cavity volume and Axis-Aligned Bounding Box cut volume. Field validation achieved 92–97% accuracy against vernier measurements, with distress classified per IRC:82–2015 to prioritise repairs.
2025
LiDAR-based temporal surface damage assessment of bridge infrastructure using efficient scan area planning
V. V. Palanisamy, S. Venkatachalam | Automation in Construction | vol. 180, art. 106526
Q1 · IF 11.5
doi.org/10.1016/j.autcon.2025.106526
Aging bridges pose significant risks to public safety, and traditional inspection lacks accuracy and historical data. This paper demonstrates and validates a scan-area-planning method that minimises differences between planned and measured interpoint spacings, achieving sub-millimetre accuracy. Point clouds of bridge-pier infrastructure were collected over two years to track cracks, spalling and reinforcement corrosion, revealing a 47.2% reduction in exposed-reinforcement diameter and a 37.1% increase in vertical crack width — supporting proactive maintenance planning and resource allocation.

From scan to field: AR visualization of TLS-based as-laid utility geometry
V. V. Palanisamy, J. V. Victor, S. Venkatachalam, SH. Hsieh | International Conference on Computing in Civil Engineering (i3CE) 2026 | Songdo, Incheon, South Korea
Accepted
Accurate location knowledge for buried utilities is essential for safe excavation, maintenance and lifecycle asset management, yet CAD/GIS records frequently diverge from as-laid conditions after backfill. This study presents a reproducible LiDAR-based workflow that transforms as-laid utility geometry into augmented-reality visualisations through two complementary representations: a mesh-based pathway preserving geometric fidelity, and a parametric BIM pathway attaching structured asset metadata via selected COBie attributes. Lightweight AR assets are prepared for mobile visualisation with provisions for GNSS-based spatial anchoring; a prototype is demonstrated, with field GNSS validation and quantitative evaluation as ongoing work.
2026
Event-based subsurface utility data collection framework
V. V. Palanisamy, S. Venkatachalam | International Symposium on Automation and Robotics in Construction (ISARC) 2026 | Singapore | pp. 2585–2592
doi.org/10.22260/ISARC2026/0331
Underground utility information is frequently incomplete, inconsistent or of uncertain confidence, leading to safety risks, rework and inefficiencies. Although standards, sensing technologies and digital platforms define data-quality concepts, their application in construction workflows remains fragmented and project-specific, with limited guidance on when utility data should be collected and how confidence should inform decisions. This paper proposes an event-based collection approach that aligns subsurface information acquisition with recurring construction and maintenance events, integrating lifecycle events, data categories, technology classes and confidence considerations to support transparent, confidence-aware decisions — enabling incremental improvement of utility information over time.
2026
Immersive virtual reality for pre-construction design review: a pre–post study of constructability, maintainability and safety review beyond automated clash detection
V. V. Palanisamy, S. Venkatachalam | 43rd CIB W78 Conference on IT in Construction 2026 | New Delhi, India | Univ. of Strathclyde & IIT Delhi
BIM and automated clash detection address primarily geometric interferences, leaving experiential, human-scale issues (constructability, maintainability, safety) hard to anticipate. This exploratory pre–post study added a five-minute guided VR walkthrough to a BIM training programme for twenty government engineers reviewing a federated model of a three-storey administrative building, first through desktop BIM and Navisworks/ACC clash detection, then in VR. All six design-review confidence dimensions increased significantly after VR (Wilcoxon, p < .01; large effects r = 0.62–0.85; Cronbach's α = 0.84–0.86), and engineers identified non-clash issues — a seeded missing stair handrail (75%) and inadequate glazing-maintenance access (60%) — absent from the clash report. VR is positioned as a complement to, not a replacement for, automated clash detection.
2025
Leveraging multi-source data for enhanced utility mapping and conflict prevention in construction
V. V. Palanisamy, S. Venkatachalam | International Conference on Smart and Sustainable Built Environment (SASBE) 2025 | Lille, France | Springer LNCE, pp. 569–579
doi.org/10.1007/978-981-95-8489-5_43
Accurate mapping of underground utilities is essential to prevent conflicts, enhance safety and avoid costly delays, but early-stage planning often relies on incomplete, record-grade data. This study presents a lightweight GIS-based screening workflow that integrates Google Earth KML pipeline centrelines with CAD master-plan layouts to identify potential proximity conflicts during the concept phase, prioritising locations for targeted high-accuracy verification and complementing traditional Subsurface Utility Engineering. A case study of an institutional campus in India shows how geoprocessing of record-grade inputs produces georeferenced outputs aligned with GNSS-validated data.
2023
Demonstration of LiDAR on accurate surface damage measurement: a case of transportation infrastructure
N. Dhanani, V. V. Palanisamy, S. Venkatachalam | International Symposium on Automation and Robotics in Construction (ISARC) 2023 | Chennai, India | pp. 553–560
doi.org/10.22260/ISARC2023/0074
Accurate damage measurement of transport infrastructure is constrained by cost, safety hazards and historical-data loss. Non-contact techniques such as LiDAR help, but measurement accuracy depends strongly on scanner placement, for which minimal guidelines exist. This study develops mathematical equations relating point-cloud density to LiDAR location and an interaction diagram predicting the effective scan area with anticipated point spacing. Evaluated on an old bridge structure — straight and curved pier surfaces scanned from guided locations — the framework detected surface damage as small as 3 mm on highway-bridge components accurately.
