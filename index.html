About

What I work on

My doctoral research sits where surveying, BIM and asset management meet the problem of invisible infrastructure. I work hands-on across the full capture-to-model pipeline — field instruments, reality capture, and the data frameworks that make the records usable.

Alongside buried utilities, I have published on bridge and road asset condition, and I teach BIM for construction management as a teaching assistant. I care most about the step that is usually skipped: deciding how much a piece of subsurface data can actually be trusted, and carrying that confidence through into a working digital twin.

Research · thesis chapters

Five threads

My thesis runs from deciding what to investigate first, through how to capture it, to trusting and modelling the result as one connected city.

Prioritisation

Which ground to dig first

A method for ranking underground infrastructure investigation by risk, uncertainty and value — so limited survey budgets go where they matter.

Confidence framework

How much to trust the record

An event-based framework that scores the reliability of subsurface utility data by how, when and with what method each point was captured.

Remote sensing

Finding pipelines from above

Using satellite imagery and vegetation response to detect buried pipeline corridors across greenfield sites, before anyone sets foot on the ground.

Reality capture

Capturing the unseen

Field workflows with GPR, LiDAR, GNSS and augmented reality to locate, record and visualise buried assets with spatial accuracy.

Digital twin

One city, every asset

Tying buried utilities together with surface assets into a cross-asset urban digital twin that holds both the geometry and the confidence behind it.
